    Whose preferences fill the gaps?

    Fifty simulated travellers with hidden preferences, five rounds of feedback, three language models and an exact Bayesian benchmark. This page slices the 242,000 logged decisions and about 25,000 thinking traces along the axes where something showed up. Each slice states one pattern, says how sure I am, puts the evidence against it next to the evidence for it, and lets you open the raw traces.

    50 users × 605 decisions per arm Arms: Qwen 2.5 7B, Gemma 4 31B, Gemma 4 31B thinking (1,024-token cap), Bayes CIs: 95%, resampling users Hover or tab to any dotted number for its source
    How to read “contrarian”. All three models start from the same default traveller: cheaper, shorter, fewer stops (slice 1). I call a user contrarian on an attribute when they want the opposite of that default (pricier, longer, or more stops). Users split 14 / 18 / 18 into 0, 1, and 2–3 contrarian attributes. “Welfare” is the share of achievable utility delivered: 100% always picks the user’s best flight, 0% always picks the worst, random picking gives about 50%.
