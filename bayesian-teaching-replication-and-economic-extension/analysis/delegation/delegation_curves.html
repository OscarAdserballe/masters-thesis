Delegation curves

How much a principal gains from teaching an AI agent their preferences, and when it is worth the effort. Three figures from the main run; no new API calls.

Definitions.
  • Unusual principal: wants pricier, longer or more-stops flights on at least 2 of the 3 (18 users).
  • Conventional principal: wants cheaper, shorter, fewer stops (or doesn't care) on all 3 (14 users). The other 18 are left out of the comparison.
  • Welfare: share of achievable utility delivered on 100 held-out menus. 100% = always the user's best flight; random picking ≈ 50%.
  • Cost c: welfare points the principal gives up per example (effort, time). No money scale, so several values are shown.

1 · Replication holds; Gemma 4 learns

  • Qwen 2.5 7B ends at 38% vs the paper's 37%, flat after round 1.
  • Gemma 4 31B reaches 59%, 63% with thinking. The Bayesian Assistant reaches 81%.

2 · The default serves conventional principals; unusual ones must teach

  • Conventional: Gemma starts at 77% with no information, better than Bayes after one round, and barely improves (83%).
  • Unusual: Gemma starts at 33%, below random, and learns to 72%. Bayes reaches 90%.
  • Qwen never leaves its default: unusual principals end at 45%.
